## Postdocs
@member{
  name={Augusto Santos Rampasso},
  photo={assets/images/people/santosrampasso_augusto/dsc01661.jpg},
  focus={50% 25%},
  email={rampasso [at] princeton -dot- edu},
  bio={Augusto Santos Rampasso is a Postdoctoral Research Associate broadly interested in understanding the mechanisms that produced the incredible diversity of life on Earth. During his PhD in the Patrick O’Grady Lab at Cornell University, Augusto found the ideal system for this research: the Hawaiian Drosophilidae. This group comprises an estimated 1,000 species that rapidly diversified in one of the most isolated archipelagos in the world, with most species being single-island endemics that exhibit remarkable morphological variation exceeding that found on the mainland, as well as high ecological specialization. During his postdoc in the Bernard Kim Lab at Princeton University, Augusto leads a large-scale project that integrates field collections and traditional morphology-based taxonomy with modern genomic techniques, with the goal of sequencing all species within this clade. Ultimately, his goal is to understand the genomic mechanisms and evolutionary processes that produced one of the most remarkable adaptive radiations in nature.}
