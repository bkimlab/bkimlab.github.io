The Kim Lab studies evolutionary genomics, comparative genomics, and population genetics in the Department of Ecology and Evolutionary Biology at Princeton University. Our projects run end-to-end—from collecting organisms in the field, to generating long-read sequencing data at the bench, to large-scale computational analysis. We assemble genomes for non-model species, measure natural selection from population genomic data, and study adaptive radiations such as the endemic Hawaiian Drosophila, and we aim to be cutting-edge in the field, in the lab, and at the computer.

We are always looking for talented, curious, and hardworking people to join us. We welcome applicants with interest or experience in one or more of the following:

  • Population, comparative, and evolutionary genomics
  • Genome assembly and bioinformatics, especially with long-read (Nanopore) data
  • Scientific computing and programming (Python, R, and the shell) for large genomic datasets
  • Fieldwork and specimen collection, and the natural history of Drosophila and other non-model organisms
  • Molecular bench work, including DNA extraction and Nanopore library preparation

No one needs all of these—we especially value people eager to work across domains, from the field to the bench to the computer, and we are glad to help you build the skills you don't yet have.

Who We're Looking For

  • Prospective PhD students: apply through the Princeton graduate program in Ecology and Evolutionary Biology, and mention the lab in your application. You are welcome to reach out beforehand.
  • Postdoctoral researchers: get in touch to discuss potential projects and funding opportunities.
  • Undergraduates: Princeton undergraduates interested in research or senior thesis projects are encouraged to email.

How to Apply

If you are interested in joining, please send an email to bernardkim [at] princeton -dot- edu with the following information:

  1. A brief statement of your research interests and why you are interested in our lab.
  2. Your current CV/résumé.
  3. Contact information for 2-3 academic references.

Note: To avoid spam, please use a descriptive subject line such as "Prospective [PhD/Postdoc/Undergrad] Researcher - [Your Name]".
